A percentage is a dimensionless mathematical ratio expressed as a fraction of 100, symbolized by the percent sign (%). The term originates from the Latin per centum, meaning "by the hundred." Percentages provide a universal benchmark to compare proportional quantities, relative price adjustments, interest rates, and statistical trends across disparate scales.

This calculator executes verified mathematical formulas instantaneously as you type:

  • Mode 1 (Percentage of a Number): (X / 100) × Y — Divides the percentage rate by 100 to find the decimal multiplier, then multiplies by the base value.
  • Mode 2 (What Percentage is X of Y): (X / Y) × 100 — Calculates the ratio of the part to the whole and multiplies by 100 to return the percentage.
  • Mode 3 (Percentage Change): ((New - Initial) / |Initial|) × 100 — Evaluates the difference relative to the starting baseline. Positive values represent an increase; negative values represent a decrease.

  • Example 1 (Discount): What is 25% off an $80 jacket? → (25 / 100) × 80 = $20 discount. Final price is $80 - $20 = $60.
  • Example 2 (Test Score): What percentage is 42 out of 50? → (42 / 50) × 100 = 84%.
  • Example 3 (Price Increase): A rent price increases from $1,200 to $1,320. → ((1,320 - 1,200) / 1,200) × 100 = +10% increase.
  • Reversing the Baseline in Percentage Change: Always divide by the original starting value, not the final value. Changing from 100 to 125 is a +25% increase, but going from 125 down to 100 is a -20% decrease.
  • Confusing Percentages with Percentage Points: An increase from 10% to 15% is a 5 percentage point gain, but a 50% relative increase.

Calculations use standard 64-bit IEEE 754 floating-point arithmetic. While accurate for everyday retail, budgeting, and study tasks, results are rounded to two decimal places. For high-stakes institutional accounting, commercial tax filing, or mortgage underwriting, always cross-reference results with certified financial software.

  • Division by Zero: When using the "is what % of Y" mode, field B cannot be 0, as division by zero is mathematically undefined.
  • Negative Baseline: When calculating percentage change with a starting value of 0, the percentage change is mathematically indeterminate.
