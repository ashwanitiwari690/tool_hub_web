A percentage is a dimensionless mathematical ratio expressed as a fraction of 100, symbolized by the percent sign (%). The term originates from the Latin per centum, meaning "by the hundred." Percentages provide a universal benchmark to compare proportional quantities, relative price adjustments, interest rates, and statistical trends across disparate scales.

  • 1. Finding X% of Y:
    Formula: Result = (X / 100) × Y
    Example: What is 20% of $85? → (20 / 100) × 85 = 17.
  • 2. Finding What Percent X is of Y:
    Formula: Result = (X / Y) × 100
    Example: You scored 45 points out of 50 on an exam. What percentage is that? → (45 / 50) × 100 = 90%.
  • 3. Calculating Percentage Change (Increase or Decrease):
    Formula: Result = ((New Value - Original Value) / |Original Value|) × 100
    Example: A stock price increases from $40 to $52. → ((52 - 40) / 40) × 100 = +30% increase.
    Example: A gadget price drops from $100 to $75. → ((75 - 100) / 100) × 100 = -25% discount.
  • Shopping Discounts: Calculating seasonal markdowns (e.g., "Take an extra 30% off clearance items").
  • Restaurant Tipping: Computing gratuity percentages (15%, 18%, 20%) on dining totals.
  • Sales Tax & GST: Calculating regional consumption tax additions before checkout.
  • Business Finance: Measuring gross profit margins, annual revenue growth rates, and customer churn.
  • Academic Grades: Converting assignment scores and weighted test results into overall course grade percentages.

This calculator executes standard IEEE 754 floating-point arithmetic. While accurate for everyday retail, budgeting, and study tasks, results are rounded to two decimal places. For high-stakes institutional finance, commercial taxation, or loan underwriting, always cross-reference results with certified financial professionals.
