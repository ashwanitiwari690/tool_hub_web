Image compression reduces the byte size of graphics and photographic files while retaining acceptable visual fidelity. Uncompressed photos captured by smartphones often weigh between 4 MB and 15 MB, making them far too heavy for smooth web loading, email attachments, and fast mobile browsing. By eliminating redundant color data and optimizing pixel encoding, compression can shrink file sizes by 60% to 80% without noticeable loss of quality on screens.

  • Lossy Compression (JPEG & WebP): Discards imperceptible color variations and high-frequency visual details that the human eye rarely notices at normal zoom levels. This yields the highest file size savings and is standard for photography and web publishing.
  • Lossless Compression (PNG): Restructures image data without discarding any pixel information. Ideal for logos, technical diagrams, screenshots, and artwork containing text, where every pixel must remain pristine.
  • JPEG (.jpg): The universal standard for photography across all hardware and legacy software. Recommended when you must email photos to users with older devices.
  • WebP (.webp): The modern standard developed by Google. Supported by over 97% of modern browsers, WebP delivers files 25%–35% smaller than JPEG at equal visual quality, making it the superior choice for website publishing and SEO performance.
  • PNG (.png): Use PNG when your graphic contains transparent cutout areas or sharp vector-like linework. Convert to WebP if you want transparency with a smaller file size.
