Image resizing alters the physical pixel dimensions (width and height) of a digital picture. When photographs are taken on modern smartphones or digital cameras, they often measure 4,000 to 6,000 pixels wide. Displaying such massive images inside a 400-pixel card on a website wastes user cellular bandwidth and harms site performance. Resizing downscales the pixel matrix so the graphic loads quickly and renders crisply at its intended display dimensions.

An aspect ratio is the proportional relationship between an image's width and its height (e.g., 16:9 for widescreen video, 1:1 for square avatars). Keeping the "Lock aspect ratio" option checked ensures that when you adjust the width, the height updates automatically in exact proportion. If the aspect ratio is unlocked and unequal dimensions are entered, the image will appear squashed, stretched, or unnaturally distorted.

Here are commonly requested digital dimension standards for online publishing:

  • Instagram Square Post: 1080 × 1080 px (1:1)
  • Instagram Story / Reel: 1080 × 1920 px (9:16)
  • YouTube Video Thumbnail: 1280 × 720 px (16:9)
  • Facebook / LinkedIn Shared Link Preview: 1200 × 630 px (1.91:1)
  • X (Twitter) Feed Image: 1200 × 675 px (16:9)
  • Website Favicon: 32 × 32 px or 64 × 64 px

Downscaling: Reducing an image from larger dimensions to smaller dimensions produces clean, razor-sharp results because the browser interpolates rich existing color data into a tighter grid.

Upscaling: Stretching an image from smaller dimensions into larger dimensions cannot invent missing detail. Upscaling will almost always introduce noticeable pixelation, blurriness, and fuzzy artifacts. For best results, always start with a high-resolution source file.
