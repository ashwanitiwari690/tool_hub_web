A Quick Response (QR) code is a two-dimensional matrix barcode invented in 1994 by Denso Wave. Unlike traditional linear barcodes that encode numbers horizontally, QR codes encode binary information both horizontally and vertically across a grid of black squares on a white background. This allows QR codes to store hundreds of alphanumeric characters in a compact, scannable format.

QR codes utilize a robust combination of geometric positioning and mathematical error correction:

  • Position Detection Patterns: The three distinctive square markers located at the upper-left, upper-right, and lower-left corners allow optical camera sensors to detect the orientation, angle, and tilt of the code instantly.
  • Data Matrix: The internal grid represents binary data (0s and 1s) arranged using standardized character encodings (Numeric, Alphanumeric, Byte, or Kanji).
  • Reed-Solomon Error Correction: Embeds mathematical redundancy into the matrix, enabling smartphones to successfully decode the payload even if up to 30% of the graphic is damaged, scratched, or obscured.
