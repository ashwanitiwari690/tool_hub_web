A Quick Response (QR) code is a two-dimensional matrix barcode invented in 1994. Unlike traditional linear UPC barcodes that store only a short sequence of numbers, a QR code can store hundreds of alphanumeric characters in a compact grid of dark modules on a light background.

Equipped with built-in position markers and Reed-Solomon error correction algorithms, modern smartphones can scan and decode a QR code from virtually any orientation, even if the code surface is partially scratched, soiled, or smudged.

Many online QR generators create "dynamic" QR codes that redirect scanners through a proprietary third-party tracking server. After a 14-day free trial, those companies disable your QR code unless you pay an expensive monthly subscription fee.
