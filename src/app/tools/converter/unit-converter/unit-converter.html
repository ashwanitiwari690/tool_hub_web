Unit conversion is the process of translating a numerical measurement from one system of measurement to another equivalent quantity in a different unit. Globally, two primary systems dominate: the International System of Units (SI / Metric), used by science, medicine, and most of the world (meters, kilograms, Celsius), and the US Customary / Imperial system, used primarily in the United States and the United Kingdom (inches, feet, pounds, Fahrenheit).

  • Length: 1 meter = 3.28084 feet • 1 kilometer = 0.621371 miles • 1 inch = 2.54 cm (exact)
  • Mass / Weight: 1 kilogram = 2.20462 pounds • 1 pound = 453.592 grams • 1 ounce = 28.3495 grams
  • Temperature: 0°C = 32°F (freezing) • 100°C = 212°F (boiling) • 20°C = 68°F (room temperature)
  • Digital Data: 1 Megabyte (MB) = 1,000 Kilobytes (KB) • 1 Gigabyte (GB) = 1,000 MB

Unlike distance or weight where zero in one unit equals zero in another (0 meters = 0 feet), temperature scales do not share a common zero point. Celsius defines zero as the freezing point of water, Fahrenheit sets freezing at 32 degrees, and Kelvin sets zero at absolute zero (-273.15°C).

Converting temperature therefore requires affine equations rather than simple multiplication:

  • Celsius to Fahrenheit: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32
  • Fahrenheit to Celsius: °C = (°F - 32) × 5/9
  • Celsius to Kelvin: K = °C + 273.15
  • Culinary Arts & Baking: Converting international recipes between grams, ounces, kilograms, and pounds.
  • International Travel & Navigation: Translating road distances and speed limits between kilometers and miles.
  • Engineering & Construction: Translating architectural measurements between centimeters/meters and inches/feet.
  • Computing & IT: Sizing database storage volumes and network bandwidth between KB, MB, and GB.

Calculations utilize high-precision international standards and are rounded to six decimal places for clarity. While suitable for academic study, DIY projects, cooking, and everyday tasks, critical pharmaceutical preparation, aerospace calculations, and legal commerce measurements should always be performed with certified instrumentation.
